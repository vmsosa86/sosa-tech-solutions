What is an AI spokesperson video?

It's a video with a presenter who looks and sounds like a real person but was generated with AI. Here's what goes into one when I make it:

  • A realistic AI presenter that fits your business and your customers.
  • A script you approve word for word. The presenter says only what you signed off on.
  • Burned-in captions, so the message comes through even with the sound off.
  • Motion graphics that put your service, your offer or your phone number on screen.
  • Vertical format for Instagram and Facebook Reels and Stories.
  • English and Spanish versions written separately. The Spanish one is written for Hispanic customers in Miami, not translated line by line from the English.

Two things it is not: it's not a "clone" of you, and it's not a real person. It's a presenter made with AI, and we say so.

When should I NOT use an AI presenter?

Here's where I'd tell you no:

  • Customer testimonials or reviews. A testimonial has to come from a real customer saying what they really think. The FTC's rule bans fake reviews and testimonials, including ones that pretend to be from someone who doesn't exist.
  • Anything that has to be you. Your personal story, an apology to a customer, a personal guarantee. People deserve to hear that from the owner.
  • Sensitive topics. Health outcomes, legal or financial advice, emergencies. Those need a real, qualified person.
  • Anything that suggests a real employee or customer said it. The presenter speaks for the business, not as "Maria from the front desk."
  • A real person's face or voice without permission. The FTC says using a celebrity avatar to praise a product without the celebrity's permission would violate its rule if people would reasonably think the celebrity actually gave that testimonial, and Florida law prohibits using a person's name or likeness in advertising without their consent.

Do I have to say it's AI? What do Instagram, Facebook and the FTC require?

Short answer: for realistic AI video on Instagram and Facebook, yes, use the label. Here's what the official sources say:

  • Meta (Instagram and Facebook). Meta requires people to use its AI disclosure tool when they post organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and it may apply penalties to people who don't. Meta also adds an "AI info" label when it detects industry-standard signs that content was made with AI, or when the person posting discloses it. On ads, Meta labels images created or significantly edited with AI, with "AI info" in the "About this ad" screen, and ads about social issues, elections or politics have their own mandatory AI disclosure.
  • The FTC. Its Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule has no blanket ban on AI-generated avatars in marketing. But fake or false testimonials are prohibited, and the FTC warns that avatars can still be deceptive under the FTC Act. Its endorsement guidance sums it up: endorsements must be honest and not misleading.
  • New York. A New York law (S8420-A), now signed and in effect, requires commercial ads to conspicuously disclose when they include a "synthetic performer": an AI-generated person who isn't a recognizable real performer. Penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each one after that. Audio-only ads are exempt. If your ads reach people in New York, keep it in mind.
  • Florida. Florida's AI disclaimer law (s. 106.145) is about political ads, not regular business ads.

My own practice is simpler than any of these rules: I label every AI video. It says so on screen and in the caption. On Instagram and Facebook, the post should also carry Meta's AI label, since its rules ask for it on realistic AI video. Nobody should have to guess.

This is general information, not legal advice. For a specific situation, talk to a lawyer.

Will labeling it as AI hurt my reach?

Honest answer: I haven't seen Meta publish anything saying a correct AI label lowers reach, and I'm not going to guess.

What Instagram has said points the other way. Its "AI-generated profile" label is for profiles that feature an AI-generated person, and Instagram warns that creators who don't label those profiles appropriately may see limits to their reach. That's about whole profiles, not single posts, but the risk Instagram has named is not labeling. Being upfront is the safer path, and it's the right one for your customers.

What's off-limits is fake or false testimonials, using a real person's name or likeness without consent, and anything that misleads people. Meta requires its AI disclosure tool for realistic AI video in organic posts, and New York requires ads to disclose synthetic performers. This is general information, not legal advice.
