AI Agent for WhatsApp: Answer Customers 24/7 Without Losing the Personal Touch

Sosa Tech Solutions

An AI agent for WhatsApp answers your customers right away, 24/7, with the information you approved, collects their details and hands the chat to you or your team when a person is needed. It's 10:40 p.m. and someone writes "¿cuánto cuesta?" or "do you have an opening tomorrow?": the agent answers instead of leaving them waiting until morning. I set these up for small businesses in Miami, in English and Spanish.

What is an AI agent for WhatsApp?

It's an assistant connected to your business WhatsApp that replies using only the information you approve: hours, services, prices, policies, service area. It understands questions written the way people actually write, in English, Spanish or a mix of both.

That's different from:

  • An away message sends one fixed text ("We're closed, we'll reply tomorrow"). The customer still waits.
  • A button bot makes people pick 1, 2 or 3, and it gets stuck the moment someone asks something that isn't on the menu.
  • An AI agent reads the actual question, answers from your approved info, asks for the details you need and is set up to bring in a person when it should.

Can AI answer my WhatsApp 24/7?

Yes, within the limits you set. On its own, it can handle:

  • Hours, location and parking
  • What you offer and what's included
  • The prices or price ranges you approve
  • Booking or estimate requests (it collects the details, you confirm)
  • Repeat questions like "do you work Saturdays?" or "can I ship a box from here?"

It's on around the clock, but it's still software: it can misread a question, and the platforms it runs on can have outages. That's why the handoff rules and reviewing its answers with you matter.

WhatsApp allows this. Its Business Messaging Policy lets businesses use automation when replying inside the 24-hour window that opens when a customer writes to you. Under Meta's per-message pricing, the first 1,000 of those replies each month are free for each business phone number; after that, they're charged too.

What does it hand off to a person?

A good agent knows when to step aside. In the setups I build, these go to you or your team:

  • Complaints or an upset customer
  • Custom quotes that need your judgment
  • Anything not covered in the approved info (it's set up not to guess)
  • Payment problems
  • Urgent or sensitive situations
  • Anyone who asks for a human

On top of that, the WhatsApp policy says businesses that automate must also offer "prompt, clear, and direct escalation paths," like an in-chat transfer to a person, a phone number, email or a support form. When the handoff happens, you get an alert with the details the agent already collected, not a blank chat.

How does it collect the customer's details?

Like a good front desk, it asks only what you need. Depending on your business, that can be:

  • Name and best way to reach them
  • What they need (service, product, trip, job)
  • Preferred date and time
  • A photo of the job, when that helps you quote
  • Preferred language

It's set up never to ask for full card numbers, bank account numbers or ID numbers. The WhatsApp policy bars asking people to share full payment card numbers, financial account numbers, personal ID card numbers or other sensitive identifiers. The details land where you work: your WhatsApp, a Google Sheet or your CRM, if it can be connected. If you also want reminders and follow-ups wired in, that's my WhatsApp & follow-up automation work, and I wrote more about it in WhatsApp automation for Miami businesses.

Is AI even allowed on WhatsApp? I heard Meta banned chatbots.

Section 4.7 of the Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform bars AI providers when AI is the primary thing being offered, as Meta determines. TechCrunch reported it as a ban on general-purpose chatbots that took effect January 15, 2026 and doesn't affect businesses using AI to serve their own customers. The same terms say a business may still hire an AI provider as its solution provider, as long as WhatsApp data isn't used to train or improve AI models (fine-tuning only for your own exclusive use is allowed).

Meta has the final say on its own terms, so I won't call that a guarantee. But an agent that serves your own customers is exactly the use reported as unaffected.

WhatsApp Business app or WhatsApp Business Platform: which one do I need?

The WhatsApp Business app is the one many small businesses already have on their phone. It has greeting and away messages, and Meta is adding its own AI, Meta Business Agent. Before you turn it on, know the trade-offs from WhatsApp's Help Center:

  • It's available only in limited countries to select businesses, with a waitlist. Check Tools > AI agent in your app.
  • It may limit broadcast lists, disappearing messages, and greeting and away messages (deleting the agent restores them).
  • It disconnects your existing linked devices. Most can be re-linked afterward.
  • Meta still collects the messages in those chats to improve its AI, even when the agent is muted.
  • WhatsApp itself warns AI messages may not be accurate.

The Platform version of Meta Business Agent also excludes some industries, including health, finance, alcohol and gambling.

The WhatsApp Business Platform (the API) is where a custom agent lives: your approved answers, your handoff rules, connections to your sheets or CRM, and approved templates for follow-ups.

Can my agent message customers first, like reminders or promos?

Only with permission. WhatsApp requires opt-in before you message someone, and Meta's opt-in guidance says it must clearly name your business. Outside the 24-hour window, you can only send approved templates, each categorized as marketing, utility or authentication. You also have to honor every opt-out, whether the person asks on WhatsApp or anywhere else, and follow applicable law.

Should customers know they're talking to AI?

My answer is yes. The agent introduces itself as your business's virtual assistant, and the customer can ask for a person at any point in the chat. That's my practice; WhatsApp's Business Messaging Policy doesn't require it. The FTC has made clear that existing consumer-protection law applies to AI too, and honesty is simply better for trust.

Is it a good fit for my business?

A good fit if:

  • You answer the same questions every day
  • Messages come in at night and on weekends
  • Your customers write in English and Spanish
  • You book appointments or send estimates

Not yet, if: you get only a few messages a week, or every chat needs your personal judgment from the first line.
